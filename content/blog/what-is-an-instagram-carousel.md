You've seen the little dots under a post, or the "1/8" in the corner, and swiped through without really thinking about what you were looking at. That format has a name, a set of rules, and a reason it keeps showing up more than any other post type in your feed. If you're about to make your first one, it's worth understanding what you're actually working with before you open a design tool.

## What Is an Instagram Carousel?

An Instagram carousel is a single post made up of multiple photos or videos, up to twenty of them, that people swipe through horizontally instead of scrolling past. Small dots appear under the post to signal there's more to see, and a "1/10" style counter shows in the corner so viewers know how far they are into it.

Unlike a single image or a Reel, a carousel is built to be consumed in sequence. The first slide is the hook. Every slide after it either earns another swipe or loses the viewer, which is a very different design problem than a single photo has to solve.

## A Quick History: From 10 Slides to 20

Instagram wasn't the first place multi-image posts existed, but it's the platform that made the swipeable carousel a mainstream format. [TechCrunch's original coverage from February 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/02/22/instagram-carousels/) describes the feature launching with a 10-photo-or-video cap, pitched explicitly as a way to stop forcing people to pick "the single best photo" from an experience worth remembering.

That 10-slide cap held for years. Instagram doubled it to 20 slides in 2024, and the format has only grown more central to how the app distributes content since. If a guide you're reading still talks about a 10-slide maximum, it's out of date.

The expansion wasn't a random product tweak. It followed years of carousels consistently beating single posts on engagement and saves, so doubling the ceiling gave creators more room in the format that was already working, rather than pushing them toward a new one. Worth noting: this is an organic-post limit. Carousel ads run through Meta Ads Manager and are still capped at 10 cards, so don't assume an ad carousel can match what you post organically.

## What Formats Does a Carousel Support?

A carousel can mix photos and videos freely within the same post, not just one or the other. Each photo slide can run up to 30MB, and each video slide can run up to 60 seconds, according to [Socialrails' 2026 breakdown of carousel specs](https://socialrails.com/blog/instagram-carousel-posts-guide). You're not limited to an all-photo or all-video post; a product carousel might open with a video demo and close with three still images, for example.

Most of the carousels writers make, thread breakdowns, quote slides, screenshot-style cards, are entirely photo-based, and that's not a limitation. Photo slides load faster, let people linger and re-read at their own pace, and are easier to screenshot and share individually, something a video slide can't offer. Reach for video slides when motion genuinely adds something (a demo, a before-and-after transition), not as a default, since a mixed carousel takes more production time for a format where text-based slides already perform well.

The one rule that catches people out: whatever aspect ratio your first slide uses becomes the aspect ratio for the entire carousel. A 4:5 portrait first slide forces every later slide to crop to 4:5, even if you uploaded it as square or landscape. There's no per-slide override, so get slide one right before you build the rest.

## Instagram Carousel Limits You Need to Know

The technical range is 2 to 20 slides per carousel, combining photos and videos in any mix, according to [bestcontent.ai's carousel reference guide](https://bestcontent.ai/blog/article/instagram-carousel). A single image isn't a carousel; you need at least two slides for Instagram to treat the post as one.

A few limits worth knowing before you start designing:

- **Slide count:** 2 minimum, 20 maximum, for posts made directly in the app.
- **Third-party schedulers cap out at 10 slides**, because that's the limit of Meta's public API, not Instagram's own app limit. If you're scheduling ahead through a tool rather than posting live, plan for 10, not 20.
- **Aspect ratio is locked to slide one** across the whole post, as covered above.
- **Recommended dimensions** are 1080 by 1350 pixels for the 4:5 portrait ratio that takes up the most space in the feed; we cover the full sizing breakdown, including square and ad formats, in [our dedicated carousel size guide](https://notes2pic.com/blog/instagram-carousel-size).
- **You can delete a slide after publishing**, but you can't reorder the slides that remain. Get the sequence right before you post.

## Carousel vs Single Post vs Reel vs Story: What's the Difference?

Each format asks something different of the algorithm, and of the person looking at it. Here's how they compare structurally, not just anecdotally.

| Format | Content per post | Best suited for | How the algorithm treats it | Typical lifespan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carousel | Up to 20 photos/videos | Step-by-step ideas, before/after, "swipe to see" stories | Re-shown to people who didn't finish swiping the first time | Days, thanks to re-serving |
| Single image | 1 photo | A punchy, single idea | One pass through the feed, no second chance | Hours |
| Reel | 1 video, roughly 90 seconds | Reaching people who don't follow you yet | Distributed broadly, including outside your followers | Minutes to hours of primary reach, longer tail via Explore |
| Story | 1 photo or video | Casual, behind-the-scenes updates | No algorithmic reach beyond your existing followers | 24 hours, then gone |

The short version: Reels are built for discovery, Stories are built for intimacy with people who already follow you, and carousels sit in between, built to deepen engagement with an idea over several seconds instead of one glance.

## Why Do Carousels Outperform Other Formats?

Two mechanics do most of the work: dwell time and the re-serve. Every swipe inside a carousel counts as continued engagement, and Instagram reads longer time-on-post as a signal the content is worth showing to more people, per [Caroubolt's 2026 carousel guide](https://caroubolt.com/en/blog/what-is-instagram-carousel). If a viewer scrolls past without swiping, Instagram can show the post again later, starting from slide two, a second chance no other format gets.

The same source cites data from Later putting carousels at roughly 2.3 times more likely to be saved than single images. Saves matter more than likes to the algorithm because they're a stronger signal of long-term value, not just a passing reaction. Comments follow a similar pattern: a carousel that unpacks an idea over several slides naturally invites people to react to a specific point ("slide 4 hit different") rather than the post as a whole, which produces more specific, higher-quality comments than a caption asking "thoughts?" under a single image ever does. That specificity is itself a weak signal back to the algorithm that the content is worth another look.

## The Format in One Sentence

A carousel is a swipeable, up-to-20-slide post that rewards structured thinking, gets a second chance in the feed that other formats don't, and works best for anyone who already writes in steps, lists, or sequences rather than single punchy lines. If that describes how you already write, the format isn't something you need to learn so much as something you need to stop leaving on the table.

## FAQ

### How many photos or videos can you put in one Instagram carousel?

Between 2 and 20, mixed freely between photos and videos. The limit was 10 until 2024, when Instagram doubled it.

### Can a carousel mix photos and videos in the same post?

Yes. There's no requirement that a carousel be all-photo or all-video; you can alternate freely as long as each slide fits the aspect ratio locked in by slide one.

### Do carousels get more reach than Reels?

Not usually. Reels are built for reaching people outside your existing followers, while carousels tend to win on engagement and saves among people who already follow you. They're solving different problems, not competing on the same metric.

### Why does my carousel look cropped weird on some slides?

Instagram locks every slide in a carousel to the aspect ratio of the first slide. If you mixed square and portrait images, the outliers get cropped to match slide one, not the other way around.

### Is a carousel the same as a Facebook or LinkedIn carousel?

The swipe mechanic is similar, but the specs differ by platform. LinkedIn and Facebook have their own slide limits and dimensions, so a carousel built for Instagram won't necessarily upload cleanly elsewhere without resizing.

### Do you need a business or creator account to post carousels?

No. Any Instagram account, personal, creator, or business, can post a carousel with up to 20 slides directly from the app. Account type affects things like insights and scheduling access, not the ability to post the format itself.

### What happens if I only upload one photo instead of two or more?

Instagram treats it as a regular single-image post, not a carousel. You need at least two slides for the dots and counter to appear and for the format's re-serve behavior to apply.
