You've been posting for months, maybe years. A handful of tweets actually landed — real engagement, replies that meant something, the odd one that got quote-tweeted by someone bigger than you. But they're scattered across your timeline, disconnected, easy to miss if someone lands on your profile today. Showcasing them together means digging through months of posts, screenshotting each one by hand, and somehow making them look like they belong in the same post — even though none of them were written to sit next to each other.

It's a genuinely useful format, and it's mostly ignored because the manual version of it — hunting for old tweets, screenshotting each, wrestling them into one Canva file — feels like more effort than it's worth for a single Instagram post. The curation is the hard part. The compilation shouldn't be.

## What Does It Mean to Turn Your Best Tweets Into a Carousel?

**Turning your best tweets into a carousel means compiling several standalone, high-performing posts — not one connected thread — into a single multi-slide Instagram post.** This is a different job than [converting an existing thread into a carousel](/blog/twitter-thread-to-instagram-carousel): a thread already has order and continuity built in. A "best tweets" carousel doesn't. You're the one deciding which posts deserve a second life and in what order they should appear, which makes curation and consistency the actual work,not splitting text.

It's a useful format for a monthly recap, an anniversary post, a "if you're new here" pinned carousel, or just a way to give your strongest one-liners more shelf life than a single scroll-by ever gave them.

## How Do You Find Your Best-Performing Tweets?

Start with X's own account analytics, which surfaces your top tweet and top media tweet automatically each month — no manual digging required for a rough first pass. One thing worth knowing before you go looking: the full desktop analytics dashboard now sits behind X Premium, so if you're on a free account, you won't get the detailed month-by-month breakdown the way you used to.

You still have free options:

- **Per-tweet mobile check** — tap the bar graph icon under any individual tweet in the X app for its impressions, engagements, and engagement rate, no subscription required.
- **The Likes tab** — a quick, rough gut-check of which tweets people responded to, sorted chronologically rather than by performance.
- **Your own memory** — if you've been posting consistently, you likely already know which three or four tweets got real replies and shares. Trust that instinct as a starting filter before you go hunting for data.

If you want a more rigorous pass, a proper workflow is to pull your full post history into a sortable table and isolate the top 10% by engagement rate then look for what those posts have in common: format, topic, timing. That pattern is often more useful than the individual tweets themselves.

## What If You're a Smaller Account Without Much Data to Sort?

Engagement rate matters more than raw numbers here — a tweet that got 40 likes on an account that averages 5 is still a real outperformer, even if it wouldn't register on a bigger account's dashboard. Don't wait for a follower count that "justifies" a highlights carousel. If you can name three or four posts that clearly did better than your typical output — more replies, people quoting it, someone screenshotting it themselves — that's a legitimate best-of list regardless of the absolute numbers behind it.

This is also a good moment to widen the definition of "best" beyond pure engagement. A tweet that didn't go anywhere in the moment but that you still think is some of your sharpest writing is a fair inclusion in a personal highlights reel — you're not obligated to let the algorithm be the only judge of what's worth resurfacing.

## How Many Tweets Should You Include in One Carousel?

Instagram carousels support [up to 20 slides](/blog/instagram-carousel-size), but a "best of" compilation reads better tighter than that. Five to nine tweets is the sweet spot for most creators — enough to feel like a genuine highlights reel, not so many that slide seven starts to feel like filler. If you're struggling to cut a longer list down, that's usually a sign you're including tweets because you like them, not because they actually performed — go back to the engagement data and let it make the harder calls for you.

## How Should You Order Them for Maximum Swipe-Through?

Lead with your strongest hook, not your most recent post. Unlike a thread, where slide one sets up a question the rest of the carousel answers, a "best of" compilation has no natural narrative order — so the job of slide one is purely to earn the swipe. Put your single best-performing tweet first, then let the rest fall into a rough rhythm: strong, medium, strong, medium, closing on your second-best. Ending on a flat note after four strong slides is the most common way these compilations lose momentum before the CTA.

Say you've picked seven tweets ranked 1 (best) through 7 (weakest) by engagement. A flat ranking-order carousel — best to worst — trains people to stop swiping the moment the quality dips, usually around slide four or five. A better order looks like: 1, 4, 2, 6, 3, 7, 5 — front-loading strength, breaking up the dips with mid-tier posts, and closing on something stronger than the slide before it rather than your literal weakest pick.

## Should You Screenshot the Original Tweets or Recreate Them as Branded Cards?

Recreate them. [Raw screenshots](/blog/screenshot-a-tweet) work fine for a single post, but a compilation carousel is stitching together tweets from different months — different timestamps, sometimes different profile photos if you've rebranded since, inconsistent surrounding UI chrome. Screenshotted side by side, a "greatest hits" carousel ends up looking like a scrapbook instead of a cohesive post. Recreating each tweet as text on one consistent branded template removes all of that noise — every slide shares the same type, spacing, and signature, even though the words behind them were written months apart.

## Should Every Slide Look Identical, or Can You Vary the Template?

Keep the core brand elements fixed — font, avatar, handle placement, color palette — but a small amount of variation between slides actually helps rather than hurts. Alternating a dark and light background every other slide, for instance, gives the carousel a visual rhythm without breaking the sense that it's one cohesive post. What you want to avoid is variation in the elements that signal consistency: if your handle moves from the top-left on slide one to the bottom-right on slide five, it reads as sloppy rather than intentional. Vary the mood, keep the structure identical.

## FAQ

### How often should you post a "best tweets" recap?

Monthly or quarterly works well for most accounts, just frequent enough to stay useful as a recap, infrequent enough that it doesn't start feeling repetitive to your regular followers.

### Do you need permission to repost your own tweets as images?

No — they're your own words. The only thing worth double-checking is whether you're recreating the text as a branded card (fine) or screenshotting someone else's reply or quote-tweet alongside yours (get a quick okay first, or leave their handle out of the recreation).

### Should you keep the original posting date on each slide?

It's optional, but including a small date or "originally posted [month/year]" adds context, especially in an anniversary or milestone recap — it signals these are proven posts, not new content dressed up as a highlight.

### Can you build a "best tweets" carousel out of tweets that never got much engagement?

Yes — engagement is a useful filter, not the only valid one. If you're curating for craft rather than performance ("my sharpest one-liners this year" rather than "my most-liked tweets"), that's a legitimate version of the format, and it's worth labeling the carousel that way in your caption so the framing is honest about what you're showcasing.
