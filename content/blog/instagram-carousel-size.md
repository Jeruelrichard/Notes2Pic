## What Size Should an Instagram Carousel Be?

**The best Instagram carousel size in 2026 is 1080 × 1350 pixels — a 4:5 portrait ratio that fills more of the feed than any other format.** Instagram supports three carousel aspect ratios: portrait (4:5, 1080×1350), square (1:1, 1080×1080), and landscape (1.91:1, roughly 1080×566). Whichever you choose, every slide in the carousel has to match it. We'll talk more on why that matters below.

Portrait is the default recommendation for most carousel content: thread breakdowns, quote cards, and step-by-step posts, where you want the first slide to dominate the screen and earn the swipe. Square is the safer, more conservative choice — it holds up consistently across the feed, Explore, and profile grid without odd cropping. Landscape is the least common choice for carousels and mostly suits panoramic or side-by-side visual content rather than text-heavy posts.

## Why Does Portrait (4:5) Outperform Square for Carousels?

Portrait carousels win the scroll because they physically take up more vertical space. Estimates on exactly how much vary by source — [TryMyPost puts it at roughly 35% more vertical screen space than a square post](https://www.trymypost.com/blog/instagram-carousel-algorithm-2026-guide), while [Overvisual estimates the gain closer to 20%](https://www.overvisual.com/tools/instagram-carousel-size) — but the direction is consistent across every carousel-sizing guide we checked: more vertical real estate pushes competing posts further down the feed and holds attention on yours a bit longer.

That's most valuable on your first slide, since that's the one competing for the swipe in a crowded feed. If you're repurposing an [X thread into a carousel](/blog/twitter-thread-to-instagram-carousel), portrait is almost always the right call — the hook slide needs every pixel of screen it can get.

There's a tradeoff, though: portrait's extra height only helps if you actually use it. A hook slide with a single short line of text floating in a mostly empty 1080×1350 canvas wastes the exact real estate you chose the format for. Square is the better call when your content is genuinely compact — a single quote, a short observation, a screenshot-style card — because it avoids the awkward empty space a sparse portrait slide creates.

## What Happens If Your Slides Don't Match in Size?

Instagram doesn't reject mismatched slides, rather, it silently crops them. The aspect ratio of the *first* image you upload becomes the aspect ratio for the entire post, and every slide after it gets force-cropped to match. [Carouselli's sizing guide is blunt about this](https://carouselli.com/blog/instagram-carousel-dimensions): if your first slide is 4:5 and your second is 1:1, Instagram crops the square slide's top and bottom to fit — and whatever text or logo sat near those edges is gone.

This is the single most common way a carousel looks broken in the wild. A creator designs slide one carefully, then pulls slide six from an old template at a different size, uploads, and never notices the crop until a follower points it out. Say your hook slide is 1080×1350 but a mid-carousel screenshot you dropped in is 1080×1080 — Instagram won't reject it or warn you. It'll silently crop the top and bottom off that screenshot to force it into 4:5, and if there was a caption or a name near either edge, it's gone from the published post.

The fix is mechanical, not creative: export every slide from the same canvas at the same pixel dimensions before you upload anything, and treat "same size" as a hard requirement rather than a rough guideline.

## Does the Profile Grid Crop Carousels Differently Than the Feed?

Yes, and this changed recently enough that a lot of sizing advice online is out of date. Instagram's profile grid moved from square previews to a 3:4 crop, which affects how a 4:5 portrait carousel actually displays on your profile versus in someone's feed. [RecurPost's 2026 sizing guide flags this as one of the more consequential recent changes](https://recurpost.com/blog/instagram-post-size-guide/) to plan around, and [Overvisual notes that a 4:5 feed image gets cropped down to a centered 1012 × 1350 px area](https://www.overvisual.com/tools/instagram-carousel-size) once it lands on your grid.

Practically, this means: don't put anything essential — text, a face, a CTA — right at the very top or bottom edge of a portrait slide. It might read fine in the feed and still get clipped on your grid.

## Can You Use a Stories-Sized (9:16) Image in a Carousel?

No. 9:16 (1080×1920) is a Stories and Reels ratio — it isn't a supported carousel format. [Carouselli confirms Instagram will crop a 9:16 upload down to 4:5](https://carouselli.com/blog/instagram-carousel-dimensions), trimming the top and bottom of your design without asking. If you're repurposing content that started life as a [Threads post](/blog/threads-to-instagram) or a vertical [Substack Note](/blog/substack-notes-to-instagram), design directly for 4:5 or 1:1 rather than adapting a Stories template after the fact.

## Can You Mix Photo and Video Slides in One Carousel?

Yes. Instagram carousels support mixed media, and the sizing rule doesn't change. [Contentdrips' 2026 format guide notes that video slides follow the same dimensional rules as image slides](https://contentdrips.com/blog/2026/05/instagram-carousel-size-format/): export at the same pixel dimensions and aspect ratio as the rest of the post, whether that's 1080×1350 or 1080×1080. A carousel that opens on a static hook slide and drops into a short video demo partway through is a common pattern for product reveals and tutorials — just keep every slide's dimensions identical, video included.

One more spec worth knowing while you're sizing things out: carousels support up to 20 slides in a single post, [per WaveGen's 2026 carousel guide](https://wavegen.ai/instagram-carousel-size) — more room than most repurposed threads will actually need, but useful to know before you split something long.

## What Resolution and File Format Should You Export At?

Match the pixel dimensions exactly — don't just match the ratio. [Hootsuite's sizing guide recommends exporting at a width of 1080 pixels](https://blog.hootsuite.com/social-media-image-sizes-guide/), with height scaling between roughly 566px and 1350px depending on which ratio you're using. Upload something wider than 1080px and Instagram resizes it down on its own; upload something narrower and it gets scaled up, which is where blur creeps in.

Compression is the other silent quality-killer. [Carouselli's guide points out that Instagram compresses every uploaded file](https://carouselli.com/blog/instagram-carousel-dimensions) to keep load times fast — so if your source export is already low-quality or over-compressed, Instagram's pass on top of it makes the result noticeably softer than what you designed. Export as a high-quality PNG or JPG in RGB color mode, at exactly the pixel dimensions your ratio calls for, and skip the temptation to upscale a smaller file to hit the spec — it won't recover detail that isn't there.

## FAQ

### What's the safe zone for text on a 1080×1350 carousel slide?

Keep essential text and visuals roughly 40px in from the left and right edges, and be mindful of the top ~150px and bottom ~350px, which can overlap with Instagram's UI elements or get clipped depending on where the post displays. [Caroubolt's sizing guide](https://caroubolt.com/en/blog/instagram-carousel-size-guide/) puts the effective safe area at roughly 1000×1070 px centered within the full 1080×1350 canvas.

### What's the maximum number of slides in an Instagram carousel?

Up to 20 slides per carousel post. Most repurposed threads land well under that limit, but it's useful headroom if you're splitting a long newsletter or essay.

### Do I need to use the same size for every post, or can it vary by carousel?

It can vary carousel to carousel — the rule only applies *within* a single post, where every slide must match the first one you upload. You can post a square carousel today and a portrait one tomorrow without any penalty.

### Should I export carousel slides as JPG or PNG?

Either works, but PNG tends to hold up better for text-heavy slides — quote cards, thread breakdowns, anything with sharp typographic edges — since it avoids the compression artifacts JPG can introduce around fine detail. Whichever format you choose, keep it consistent across the whole carousel so Instagram's own compression pass treats every slide the same way.

### Does a single-image post follow the same sizing rules as a carousel?

The same three ratios apply — portrait, square, and landscape — but a single post has no "first slide sets the rest" problem, since there's only one image. The dimensions matter just as much for how much feed space it commands, but you don't have to worry about matching slides against each other.
