You've got a newsletter, you post consistently, and growth still feels stuck at the same slow trickle it's been at for months. Meanwhile someone with a fraction of your writing chops is picking up subscribers daily, and the difference isn't the newsletter itself. It's that they're showing up somewhere you're not: Notes.

## What Are Substack Notes?

Notes is Substack's short-form feature for publishing quick posts, links, images, and quotes to your subscribers and the wider Substack network, separate from your full newsletter posts. According to [Substack's own guide to getting started with Notes](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/14564821756308-Getting-started-on-Substack-Notes), it lives on its own Home tab where readers see short-form content from writers they subscribe to, plus content from across the broader Substack network they don't yet follow.

Functionally, it behaves like a feed inside the app you already publish to, built for the small, frequent thought that doesn't justify a full post but still deserves to exist somewhere other than your drafts folder. If you've used X or Threads, the format will feel immediately familiar: short posts, replies, likes, and shares, except every interaction happens inside a network made up of people who already read and write long-form content, not a general-purpose feed optimized for anything that keeps someone scrolling.

## How Are Notes Different From a Full Substack Post?

The core difference isn't length, technically Notes have no enforced character limit, it's purpose. A post is something a reader commits to and reads start to finish. A Note is something they scroll past or stop on in seconds, which changes what belongs in each.

| | Note | Full post |
|---|---|---|
| Typical length | Under 250 words in practice | 500 to 5,000+ words |
| Character limit | None, technically | None |
| Who sees it | Subscribers plus the wider Notes network | Subscribers, plus anyone with the link |
| Best for | A single thought, quote, or quick reaction | A developed argument or story |
| Growth mechanism | Discovery through restacks and the network feed | Discovery through search, shares, referrals |

An analysis of 1,000 published Notes found the practical sweet spot sits well under the technical maximum, [most effective Notes run under 250 words](https://thewritinglonggame.substack.com/p/i-analyzed-1000-substack-notes-heres), even though nothing stops you from writing more. Substack's own FAQ confirms the format supports [up to six images per Note, rich text formatting, and a "restack with note" feature](https://on.substack.com/p/notes-faq) that lets you share someone else's post or Note while adding your own commentary on top, which is the mechanic most growth on the platform actually runs through.

The practical test for which one you're writing: if you're explaining a single idea, reacting to something you read, or sharing a quick number or update, it's a Note. If you need supporting evidence, multiple sections, or a real argument that builds across paragraphs, it's a post, and trying to cram that into a Note usually just produces a Note nobody finishes reading.

## Where Do Notes Actually Show Up?

Beyond your own subscriber base, and that reach has gotten bigger recently, not smaller. Late in 2025, Substack changed how the Notes feed works: it stopped prioritizing content from people you already follow and started surfacing Notes from writers you've never subscribed to, based on what the algorithm thinks you'd want to see, according to [Niusleters' 2026 rundown of Substack's platform changes](https://niusleters.com/en/blog/substack-2026-whats-changed-whats-working-and-whats-not). That single change is why writers who were stuck at a plateau for months sometimes see a Note reach thousands of new accounts overnight; growth stopped depending on who already follows you and started depending on what the algorithm decides is worth amplifying.

The same source notes that visual content is climbing fast inside Notes, roughly one in three Notes now includes a photo or video, which is part of why Substack redesigned its media tab into a scrollable, Instagram-like feed. Text-only Notes still work, but a Note with an image attached has more real estate to earn a stop mid-scroll.

Practically, this means the writers still treating Notes purely as an announcement channel, "new post is up, link in bio", are leaving most of the format's actual value on the table. The algorithm change rewards Notes that stand on their own as content, not Notes that exist to point somewhere else.

## How Notes Actually Drive Subscriber Growth

Restacking, replying, and joining other people's Notes, not just posting your own. Substack has described the format as having a "dinner party" dynamic: publishing into a void gets you far less traction than actually being part of the conversation, according to [SubstackWritersAtWork's 2026 breakdown of the Notes algorithm](https://www.substackwritersatwork.com/p/the-new-substack-notes-algorithm-2026), which cites detail shared directly by Substack's Head of Machine Learning, Mike Cohen. High-signal replies, ones that genuinely add to what someone else wrote, now outrank simple reactions like hearts, and restacking other writers' work in your niche is one of the more reliable ways to get their subscribers' attention pointed back at you.

That's a meaningfully different growth model than most social platforms run on. You're not just performing for an algorithm in isolation, you're building visibility inside a network of writers who can see, restack, and reply to what you post, which compounds in a way a single viral post rarely does on its own. A writer who restacks five other people's Notes a week, with an actual comment attached rather than just a heart, tends to see more inbound attention over a month than one who only ever posts their own content and never engages with anyone else's.

## What Kind of Notes Perform Best?

Notes that teach something specific, tell a real story, or genuinely help another writer, not motivational one-liners dressed up as insight. Purely inspirational quotes tend to get quick engagement and little else; Notes that share a concrete tip, a behind-the-scenes number, or an honest account of something that didn't work are what actually convert a stranger into a subscriber, because they demonstrate the kind of thinking someone would want delivered to their inbox regularly.

The practical version of this: write the Note the way you'd explain the idea out loud to one person in under a minute, not the way you'd open a full essay. If it needs three paragraphs of setup before the point lands, it's probably a post, not a Note.

A rough rotation worth stealing from writers who post consistently: alternate between a genuine tip or insight, a specific number or behind-the-scenes update, an honest account of something that didn't go to plan, and a question that invites real replies rather than reactions. Pure promotion, "check out my new post", should be the rarest category in the rotation, not the default, since it's the one type of Note the algorithm and the audience both respond to least.

## The Short Version

Notes is Substack's short-form, network-wide feed: no strict character limit but a practical sweet spot under 250 words, discoverable well beyond your own subscribers since the 2025 algorithm change, and driven more by restacking and genuine conversation than by broadcasting into a void. Writers who treat it as a dinner party instead of a megaphone are the ones actually converting strangers into subscribers.

## FAQ

### Do Substack Notes have a character limit?

No official limit exists, but an analysis of 1,000 published Notes found the most effective ones run under 250 words. Longer Notes aren't broken, they just tend to underperform against the format's natural, quick-read rhythm.

### Can people who don't subscribe to me see my Notes?

Yes. Since Substack's 2025 algorithm change, Notes are surfaced to readers across the wider network based on relevance, not just to your existing subscribers, which is the main reason Notes has become a discovery tool rather than just an announcement feed.

### What does "restack" mean on Substack?

Restacking shares someone else's post or Note to your own audience, optionally with your own commentary added on top. It's one of the primary ways content spreads across the Notes network and a major driver of new subscriber discovery.

### Should I post Notes every day?

Consistency matters more than a specific daily quota. Writers who show up regularly and genuinely engage with other people's Notes, not just their own, tend to see steadier growth than those posting sporadically no matter how good any single Note is.

### Is a Note the same as a tweet?

Structurally similar, short-form, feed-based, built for a quick read, but the discovery mechanics differ. Notes spread through restacks and replies inside a network of writers and readers who already value long-form content, which tends to convert differently than a platform built around raw reach.

### Can I edit or delete a Note after posting it?

Yes, Notes can be edited or deleted after publishing, similar to a post. There's no time limit on when you can make changes, so a Note that needs a correction or an update doesn't have to be deleted and reposted from scratch.

### Do I need paid subscribers before Notes will work for me?

No. Notes discovery runs independently of your subscription tiers or revenue. A brand-new, entirely free publication can reach new readers through Notes just as well as an established paid one, since the algorithm surfaces content based on relevance and engagement, not subscriber count.
